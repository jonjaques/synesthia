# Synesthia 1.2

This release adds a fourth visualizer, rebuilds Nebula as a GPU simulation, and reworks how Synesthia knows what you're listening to: the now-playing badge now recognizes Music and Spotify with no permission at all, and controlling the player is a separate, optional step. The "Music app" audio source is gone — see the note under Improved.

## New

- **Bars visualizer** — a producer's console: a wall of segmented LED meters with peak-hold caps over the 64-band spectrum, two analog VU needles with a waveform scope between them, and a desk of moving faders and EQ knobs receding in perspective. Options for columns, LED segments, peak hold, glow, channel count, and a switch to hide the desk panel.
- **Now-playing badge for any player, with no permission** — the badge names the track, artist, and album for Music and Spotify, dims when the player is paused, and clicking it brings that player to the front. This works in both the App Store and direct-download builds; nothing is asked of you for it.
- **Artwork tile** — where there's no cover art, the badge draws a gradient sampled from the current visualizer's palette, stable per album, with the reporting player's icon in the corner instead of a grey square.
- **Optional player control** — "Control Music…" / "Control Spotify…" appears in the source menu and the Playback menu once a player is detected. Choosing it is what raises the macOS Automation prompt, and it adds prev/play/next plus real album artwork. Direct-download build only; the App Store build sends no Apple Events at all.
- **Normalize Loudness** — a new Settings window (⌘,) with a toggle, on by default. The analyzer slowly adapts to how loud the source actually is, so a quiet microphone and loud mastered music both fill the picture without retuning Sensitivity. Turn it off to get the old fixed response back.
- **Toggle options for visualizers** — options can now be switches, not just sliders, and a visualizer may declare up to 16 of them.

## Improved

- **Nebula rebuilt on the GPU** — roughly 130,000 particles instead of 4,000, simulated in a compute kernel rather than a loop on the render thread. Particles are pushed around by forces instead of tracking a formula, so they overshoot and stream: a pulsing bass core, a shearing galaxy disc, and a treble halo that throws comets on hi-hats, with one shockwave per kick sweeping through all three. Rendering is HDR with real bloom, so bright cores glow instead of clipping. New controls: Turbulence, Swirl, Orbit speed, Spread, Halos, Impact, and Form.
- **The "Music app" source is gone** — it was never a separate audio path (Music exposes no stream, so it was already being heard through the system-audio tap), and it forced a choice between two sources that sounded identical. Anyone whose last session used it now lands on System Audio, which behaves the same and shows the badge. Transport control moved to the opt-in above.
- **Missing permissions are explained on the canvas** — instead of a one-line yellow banner, a card appears where the visuals would be, naming the permission, what granting it buys, and offering both a direct link into System Settings and Try Again. Come back from System Settings with the box ticked and playback resumes on its own.
- **Source picker only offers what will work** — sources whose permission you haven't granted are listed separately as disabled, with a "Grant Access…" entry that reopens the welcome window. The demo track left the picker; it now lives in the welcome window and Help ▸ Play Demo Track.
- **Welcome window redesigned** — one clickable row per source stating what it shows and what it costs, plus a plain statement that nothing is recorded or uploaded, all before macOS asks for anything.
- **Clearer wording throughout** — permission explanations, capture errors, and visualizer taglines were rewritten to say what's happening rather than name the framework behind it.

## Fixed

- **System audio no longer fails the first time after you grant the permission** — a freshly granted permission can refuse the first capture attempt; that attempt is now retried automatically instead of surfacing as an error.
- **Visualizers with a broken shader fail loudly** — a missing shader function now reports which one is missing rather than producing a black canvas.
